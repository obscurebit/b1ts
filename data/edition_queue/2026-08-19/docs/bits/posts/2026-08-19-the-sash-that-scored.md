# The Sash That Scored

The sash was supposed to be a relic—ceremonial, they said, like the velvet ropes at old theaters that meant *keep out* but looked fancy doing it. Ours were teal, with silver thread that hummed when you walked past the checkpoint’s sensor array. They didn’t even track access anymore; the border algorithms handled that. The sashes were for show, for memory, for reminding us who’d owed whom since the Last Reconciliation.  

We were halfway through the monthly maintenance—Mara elbow-greasing a stain off the archive terminal, Jesse rerouting a frayed extension cord around the snack cart—when the sashes started lying.  

Not glitching. Lying.  

Mara’s went first: a jagged crimson line across her chest, pulsing like a gutstring. The thread spelled *OWES: 1 UNCONFESSED*. She froze, her knuckles white on the terminal. “Joke’s on them,” she muttered. “I haven’t kept a secret in forty years.”  

Then Jesse’s sash flickered. *OWES: 3, BUT WHO’S COUNTING?* He snorted. “Finally, some honesty.”  

Ours lit up next, all of them at once. The room filled with the whir of thread rewriting itself. Lila’s sash declared *OWES: A FAVOR TRADED*, which was fair—she’d gotten her cousin through the checkpoint last year using someone else’s clearance. Ravi’s blinked *OWES: NOTHING*, which was a lie so obvious it felt like a rebellion.  

Mara’s eyes narrowed. “This isn’t the system. This is someone’s *finger* on the system.”  

The sashes had always been dumb things, decorative. But someone—some*body*—had taught them to read the debt ledgers buried in the border’s deep code. The ones we never talked about. The ones that predated the algorithms, predated the peace.  

“Maintenance,” Mara growled, yanking the extension cord free, “is a lie.”  

We followed her to the sub-basement, where the original ledger stones were supposed to be sealed. They weren’t. Someone had pried up the tile floor, and the stones—slick black slabs that used to record crossings—were warm, active, *listening*.  

Jesse crouched. “Whoever did this… they didn’t just hook the sashes to the stones. They taught the stones to *interpret*.”  

Mara’s sash flared: *OWES: THE TRUTH, STILL*.  

The debt she wouldn’t name. The one from before the Reconciliation, when she’d smuggled a truckload of medicine past the old border guards in exchange for her brother’s life. The brother who’d burned down a checkpoint during the Uprising. The favor that made her untouchable, then irrelevant, then a custodian of relics.  

“You think the sash is judging you?” Lila said. “It’s just a mirror.”  

“It’s a *vandal*,” Mara hissed.  

But the stones weren’t done. They pulsed, and every sash in the room rewritten. Now they showed not just debts, but the *weight* of them. Ravi’s *OWES: NOTHING* dimmed to gray. Jesse’s added a footnote: *BUT YOU’LL OWE FOR THIS*.  

Mara’s sash split into two threads: *OWES: 1 UNCONFESSED* and, smaller, *DUES: 37 LIVES SAVED*.  

She stared. “That’s… not how it works.”  

“Maybe it is now,” Jesse said.  

The social worker from the Upper Administration appeared at the stairwell, clipboard in hand. She squinted at the sashes. “Ah. The new system. We were going to announce it next week.”  

Mara stepped forward, her sash throbbing. “You didn’t authorize this.”  

“No,” the woman said. “But we *noted* it. Figured it’d surface eventually.”  

The room hung. Then Jesse snorted. “You mean you let it happen because it’s easier than dealing with the backlogged grievances yourself.”  

The woman smiled like a knifethrower. “You’re perceptive. There’s a promotion in it for you.”  

Jesse’s sash flickered: *OWES: 4, INCLUDING THIS CONVERSATION*.  

We watched Mara’s sash for a full minute. It didn’t change.  

Finally, Lila said, “So what? You gonna confess the whole bloody history?”  

Mara lit a cigarette, the old kind that weren’t allowed. “I’ll burn the sash first.”  

“You’ll burn *us* first,” Ravi said. “That thing’s got all of us on it now.”  

The stones hummed approval.  

Jesse broke the silence. “Y’know what’s weird? The sashes don’t show who you owe. Just that you do. So maybe…” He gestured to Mara’s dual threads. “…the rest is on us.”  

The social worker cleared her throat. “We’ll need a report by morning.”  

As she left, Lila deadpanned, “Promotion’s gonna look great on you, Jesse.”  

He glared. “Shut up. My sash is lying for me.”  

We laughed, sharp and brief. Mara’s sash dimmed to a steady teal. Not forgiven. Not clean. But quiet.  

For now.  

“Same time tomorrow?” Jesse asked.  

“We’ll be here,” Mara said, grinding out her cigarette on the ledger stone. “Ain’t like we got clean slates.”  

The sashes flickered in agreement.
